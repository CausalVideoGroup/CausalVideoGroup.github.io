07A · Long horizon / rollout side

First control error propagation, then keep history dynamic

These methods retain the teacher and directly modify the generation process and long-term anchor.

Generation process

Rolling Forcing

Problem: strict frame-wise or chunk-wise causality immediately propagates each error to the next timestep.
Do not complete only one frame at a time: denoise multiple frames jointly in a rolling window, locally relaxing strict causality.
  • Joint multi-frame denoising within the window
  • Progressively increasing noise toward the future
  • A first-frame attention sink preserves the global anchor
  • Few-step distillation over an expanded window
Result: suppresses error growth and supports real-time, minute-scale generation
Dynamic anchor + objective

Reward Forcing

Problem: although a fixed first-frame sink is stable, it makes later frames copy the beginning and gradually lose motion.
The anchor must evolve with the video: long-term memory cannot remain fixed at the first frame forever.
  • EMA-Sink incorporates historical tokens evicted from the sliding window
  • Maintains fixed cache size while absorbing recent dynamics
  • Re-DMD reweights samples using motion rewards from a vision-language model
Result: preserves stability while reducing first-frame copying and motion decay

07B · Long horizon / supervision side

How can a short-horizon teacher train a student to generate videos tens of times longer?

Both works correct the teacher-student horizon gap, but one extracts windows for correction while the other extends the teacher context.

Long rollout → short correction

Self-Forcing++

Problem: a short-video teacher cannot directly generate or supervise a complete multi-minute trajectory.
The student travels far; the teacher only makes local corrections: extract short windows from the student's long rollout.
  • The student first generates a video beyond the training length
  • Randomly extract short clips for a short-horizon bidirectional teacher
  • The teacher corrects each local window, which is distilled back into the student
  • Backward-noise initialization: re-noise the extracted student latent while preserving its content relationship to the preceding context
Requires neither real long-video data nor a long-video teacher
Long teacher ↔ long student

Context Forcing

Problem: the teacher sees only a short clip, while the student predicts from long history, so their conditioning information differs.
The supervisor must also know the full history: give the teacher and student matching long contexts.
  • The teacher accesses long history, eliminating context mismatch
  • Slow Memory preserves sparse long-term semantics
  • Fast Memory retains recent details and motion
  • Extends the effective supervision horizon within the compute budget
Closes the information gap in short-teacher, long-student supervision
Self-Forcing++Context Forcing
07C · LongLive family / direct AR training

Another long-video lineage: train long, test long

It runs alongside the Forcing-distillation lineage but does not rely on ODE initialization followed by DMD; its progression is training horizon, system efficiency, then retrievable history.

Model & long-horizon training

LongLive

Problem: a short-clip model degrades when directly extrapolated; streaming prompt changes also contaminate stale KV states.
Train for the full duration required at test time: directly narrow the train-long-test-long gap.
  • Frame-level causal AR
  • Streaming Long Tuning
  • Short-window attention + Frame Sink
  • KV-Recache refreshes prompt conditioning
Model level: minute-scale, real-time, interactive long-video generation
Training & inference infrastructure

LongLive-2.0

Problem: long-sequence AR training consumes substantial memory and has imbalanced parallel workloads; deployment throughput remains limited by precision and the VAE.
Instead of stacking more distillation stages, co-design AR training and hardware execution.
  • Balanced Sequence Parallel teacher-forcing
  • NVFP4 training + W4A4 inference
  • NVFP4 KV cache + asynchronous streaming VAE
  • Optional LoRA converts four-step generation to two-step
System level: end-to-end acceleration for direct AR tuning
Retrieval-augmented history

LongLive-RAG

Problem: once errors accumulate in the sliding window, the model can only continue along a degraded trajectory and cannot recover earlier identities.
Turn self-generated latent history into content-addressable external memory rather than consulting only the most recent window.
  • Each new block queries relevant historical latents
  • Nonlocal retrieval results are fed back into the generator
  • Window Temporal Delta Loss suppresses local redundancy
Memory level: replaces an irreversible sliding window with retrievable history

08A · Few-step methods

The key to 1-2 steps is not fewer evaluations, but redesigned initialization

Consistency initialization

Causal Forcing++

Complete causal PF-ODE trajectories are expensive and difficult to scale to 1-2 frame-wise steps.
Learn only a short flow-map segment between adjacent timesteps, eliminating the need to store an entire ODE trajectory.
  • Causal consistency distillation
  • Online single-step teacher ODE supervision
  • Followed by asymmetric DMD
Two steps · Lower first-frame latency and training cost
One-step refinement

One-Forcing

One-step consistency tends to lack motion, while one-step DMD tends to blur.
DMD governs distribution and motion; an auxiliary GAN governs one-step sharpness.
  • Retain the self-forcing DMD primary objective
  • Add adversarial image and video signals
  • Specifically stabilize one-step AR rollout
One step · Balances sharpness and motion
Forward + reverse divergence

Causal-rCM

CM alone tends to average modes, while DMD alone lacks stable initialization.
Turn them into two stages: TF-CM establishes coverage; SF-DMD sharpens the distribution.
  • Teacher-forcing continuous-time CM
  • Self-forcing on-policy DMD
  • A unified open recipe for frame-wise and chunk-wise models
1-2 steps · Complementary initialization and distribution refinement

09A · Alternative methods

Three alternatives to the full DMD distillation pipeline

Their shared goal is to reduce reliance on a bidirectional teacher, ODE data, and an online critic.

Adversarial post-training

AAPT

The few-step diffusion-distillation pipeline is complex, and inference still requires multiple network evaluations.
Turn the causal generator directly into a 1-NFE GAN and train it on its own history.
  • Student-forcing rollout
  • Causal adversarial post-training
  • Avoids the DMD critic recipe
Trades adversarial training for one-step speed
Teacher-free training

Resampling Forcing

Distillation relies on an expensive teacher, and training conditions still struggle to reproduce inference-time errors.
Repeatedly resample the model's own outputs during training to proactively recreate the history distribution it will encounter at inference.
  • Self-resampling simulates accumulated errors
  • History routing controls the source of conditioning
  • End-to-end training from scratch
Bypasses both the teacher and online discriminator
Shared-model self-distillation

Mutual Forcing

An additional bidirectional teacher introduces architecture and capability mismatches.
Let the multi-step and few-step modes of one natively causal model teach each other.
  • Shared-parameter slow / fast generation
  • Mutual self-distillation between the few-step and multi-step modes
  • Designed for synchronized audio-video generation
Internalizes the teacher as the model's own slow mode

09B · Mutual Forcing / dual-mode loop

Fast and slow are not two models: one parameter set, two step sizes

The few-step mode reproduces the true inference history; the multi-step mode provides high-quality learning targets. Both training signals update the same parameters.

Fast · Few-step mode

A few large jumps

4–8 network evaluations

Predicts the average change across a large noise interval in one pass, rapidly jumping from noise to clean audio-video.

  • Input: noisy current block xt, generated history ĉ, interval endpoint s, and text / first-frame / speech control
  • Output: average velocity over the interval from t to s, i.e., how far one large jump should move
  • Use: generate history during training; directly generate audio-video at deployment
Supervision:
The slow mode's multi-step output, treated as a stop-gradient target; ShortCut enforces large-jump consistency, while DMD improves few-step distribution quality.

Slow · Multi-step mode

Many small steps

Denoise in small increments along the ODE path

Each prediction covers only the next short denoising segment from the current noise position, making it more precise and stable.

  • Input: xt, obtained by adding noise to the real current block x₀; fast-mode history ĉ; time t; and control signals

10A · System / sparse compute

Not all history and attention heads merit equal computation

Shared intuition: first identify attention patterns, then allocate different budgets to different chunks or heads.

Chunk-aware sparsity

Light Forcing

Generic sparse attention ignores the historical dependencies of AR chunks.
As generation proceeds, progressively increase sparsity according to each chunk's contribution.
  • Chunk-Aware Growth
  • Hierarchical Sparse Attention
  • Select masks at both frame and block levels
Preserves critical history while reducing quadratic complexity
Head-wise context

Dummy Forcing

Roughly one quarter of heads attend almost exclusively to the current frame, making historical caching wasteful.
Give these "dummy heads" short memory, while other heads retain long memory.
  • Dynamic head classification
  • Heterogeneous memory allocation
  • Context packing
Compresses the cache and accelerates inference without training
Hybrid pruning

Forcing-KV

Uniform pruning harms both image-quality heads and motion heads.
First separate their functions: static heads model structure; dynamic heads model motion.
  • Static: structured pruning
  • Dynamic: pruning by clip similarity
  • Head types remain stable across samples
Selects different KV compression strategies by function
Pyramidal cache

Pyramid Forcing

Different heads have different temporal dependency patterns, making a uniform cache length inappropriate.
Divide heads into three classes: Anchor / Wave / Veil.
  • Anchor: broad long-term history
  • Wave: periodic dependencies
  • Veil: first frame and neighboring frames
Heterogeneous lengths + ragged-cache attention

10B · System / memory policy

More long-term history is not always better: roles and forgetting matter

Structured temporal memory

Relax Forcing

Densely retaining all history is expensive and continues propagating errors and stale motion patterns.
Decompose history into three roles: stability, continuity, and motion.
  • Sink: global stability
  • Tail: short-term continuity
  • Dynamic History: structural and motion guidance
Selects relevant history instead of enlarging a homogeneous buffer
Training-free cache repair

Deep Forcing

A standard sink causes quality degradation, repetition, and motion slowdown.
The anchor should be deeper, and old history should retain only tokens that still participate in recent attention.
  • Deep Sink occupies half of the sliding window
  • RoPE phases are realigned to the current time
  • Participative Compression prunes degraded history
Extends generation by more than 12x without training
Interactive scene memory

Echo-Forcing

After a prompt switch, the old background contaminates the new scene; the model cannot recall it after switching back.
Memory must simultaneously preserve scenes, respond to changes, and actively forget.
  • Hierarchical Temporal Memory
  • Scene Recall Frames compress old scenes
  • Difference-aware Memory Decay
Supports transitions and long-range scene recall with a fixed cache

12 · Consistency / two different axes

First distinguish two meanings of consistency

One occurs along the diffusion-time axis and determines whether few-step generation is possible; the other occurs along the video-time axis and determines whether a long rollout remains coherent.

Diffusion-time consistency
τ

The same content at different noise times should map to the same result

Core question: how can 50 small denoising steps be compressed into 1-2 large steps?

Starting from different noise positions, the student should recover the same clean result corresponding to one teacher trajectory. This is the consistency studied by consistency distillation, CM, and ShortCut.

Focus: correct target · ability to cross the trajectory in one step · stable initialization
Video-time consistency
t

Within one world, consecutive frames should preserve identity, motion, and causality

Core question: why does an AR model drift after repeatedly consuming its own outputs?

People, objects, scenes, and dynamics should remain consistent over long durations, yet the model must also respond promptly to prompt changes. This is the consistency studied in exposure bias, memory, and long-context work.

Focus: history distribution · long-term memory · balance between stability and motion
The two are coupledFew-step errors enter the next frame's history; temporal drift then makes every subsequent few-step denoising operation more difficult.
12A · Diffusion-time consistency

Few-step consistency: four failure modes and current solutions

Problem 1 · Wrong target

The teacher trajectory is not causal

The bidirectional teacher uses future frames; the causal student can never reproduce the same flow map.
Solution: first obtain an AR teacher, or directly use causal CD to learn the correct AR conditional mapping.
  • Causal Forcing: causal ODE
  • Causal Forcing++: causal CD
Align the target before reducing the step count
Problem 2 · Scaling cost

Complete ODE trajectories are too expensive

Each sample requires generating and storing dozens of teacher trajectory steps; configuration changes require recomputation.
Solution: align only adjacent noise times, using one online teacher ODE step for local supervision.
  • No offline trajectory bank required
  • Smaller optimization interval at each step
Causal Forcing++ · causal CD
Problem 3 · One-step geometry

Video trajectories curve too sharply

Trajectory curvature is high in high-noise regions; a single straight-line jump loses structure and motion.
Solution: retain an intermediate anchor in a two-step scheme, or move beyond trajectory following and directly match the output distribution.
  • Two-step consistency: crosses the bend
  • One-Forcing: DMD + GAN
One-step generation requires an additional global realism constraint
Problem 4 · Objective bias

Coverage and sharpness are difficult to achieve together

CM is stable and covers modes but tends to average them; DMD is sharper but prone to mode-seeking and initialization sensitivity.
Solution: let the forward objective provide stable coverage and the reverse objective perform on-policy distribution refinement.
  • Causal-rCM: TF-CM → SF-DMD
  • Mutual: ShortCut + DMD
Not either-or: stabilize first, then sharpen

12B · Video-time consistency

Video consistency: every problem begins when history is no longer reliable

History distribution

Training history is too clean

Training sees ground truth, while inference sees the model's own error-bearing outputs, causing exposure bias.
Solution: perform an actual few-step rollout during training so the model learns to continue from its own errors.
  • Self Forcing
  • Mutual Forcing's Few-history
  • LongLive's long tuning
Aligns training history with test history
Teacher horizon

A short teacher cannot teach long dependencies

A short teacher knows only a local window, while the student must make decisions from tens of seconds of history.
Solution: let the student travel far and the teacher correct locally, or give the teacher long context as well.
  • Self-Forcing++: extract windows from a long rollout
  • Context Forcing: long-context teacher
Corrects the student-teacher context gap
Anchor trade-off

Anchors stabilize but can freeze motion

A fixed first-frame sink suppresses drift but may copy the beginning and reduce motion.
Solution: update the anchor with history and assign distinct roles to long-term, recent, and motion memories.
  • Reward: EMA-Sink + Re-DMD
  • Relax: Sink / Tail / Dynamic
  • Deep: Deep Sink + history compression
Stability and dynamism still require joint optimization
Irreversible history

No recovery after a sliding-window mistake

Once identity drifts within the recent window, the old scene has been evicted and generation can only continue along the erroneous trajectory.
Solution: turn distant history into external memory that can be selected, recalled, and forgotten.
