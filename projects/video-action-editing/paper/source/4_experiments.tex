\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate whether demonstrations transfer dynamics without appearance on
synthetic videos (Section~\ref{subsec:synthetic-experiments}) and compare
action editing with open editors on real videos
(Section~\ref{subsec:real-experiments}).

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{subsec:experimental-setup}

Synthetic clips show an actor box pushing a patient box in one of eight
directions, simulated with MuJoCo~\citep{todorov2012mujoco}; each motion
is rendered in two block appearances, giving the views of
Eq.~\ref{eq:controlled-anchor-views} (Appendix~\ref{subsec:synthetic-dataset}).
Real-world training uses 1,508 source--target pairs from VADD-Dataset
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:real-dataset}), each formed by two consecutive events
of a stock video and an instruction describing the target event, with
demonstrations mixing real targets and offline-generated references at 3:1
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:impl-configuration}). We evaluate on the 200 cases of
the OmniEdit-Bench Temporal track~\citep{miao2026omnieditbench}, whose
accuracy-gated score combines judged instruction accuracy, preservation,
realism, and consistency (Appendix~\ref{subsec:omni-evaluation}). We compare
with six open instruction-guided editors of 1.3B--5B parameters
(Appendix~\ref{sec:baselines}): VACE~\citep{jiang2025vace},
ReCo~\citep{zhang2026reco}, OmniVideo2~\citep{yang2026omnivideo2},
Kiwi-Edit~\citep{lin2026kiwiedit}, LiveEdit~\citep{wang2026liveedit}, and
Bernini~\citep{bernini2026planning}, whose public 1.3B renderer we run
frozen and use as VADD's backbone. All methods receive only the source video
and instruction; Lucy Edit~\citep{decart2025lucyedit}, an appearance editor,
appears only in Figure~\ref{fig:real-qualitative}. As a reference, Direct-FM
trains the same adapters on the same pairs by flow
matching~\citep{lipman2023flowmatching} toward the real targets, without
an action condition. At inference, Qwen3-VL~\citep{bai2025qwen3vl} rewrites
each source video and instruction as a text-to-video prompt; the frozen
backbone generates a demonstration from it, whose action anchor conditions
the edit, so no real target is read.

\subsection{Action transfer on synthetic videos}
\label{subsec:synthetic-experiments}
Synthetic videos isolate the recombination in
Eq.~\ref{eq:dgp-counterfactual}: a condition carrying the demonstration's
appearance would recolor the source blocks, and an editor ignoring the
demonstration would keep the source push. In
Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-qualitative}, the outputs follow the demonstrated
trajectories, including the initial approach and later turn on the right,
while keeping the source colors. Because the action block includes the
initial state, the initial placement also follows the demonstration. Blur
around the fast-moving actor (left, frames 27 and 53) remains.

VADD achieves the best Action and All scores in
Table~\ref{tab:omni-temporal} and improves every category over its frozen
backbone. Both share the backbone, sampler, and inputs, and VADD's
demonstrations come from the same frozen model, so the gain comes from the
learned action condition rather than a stronger generator or an external
reference. Direct-FM, trained on the same pairs, gains on motion semantics
but falls below the backbone on attribute and camera edits: fitting
consecutive-event targets favors changing the action category. Supervising
behavior through extracted action anchors avoids this trade-off and yields
a higher overall score. Temporal Composition remains hard for all methods,
since a single demonstration may not specify inserted, deleted, or
reordered events.

In Figure~\ref{fig:real-qualitative}, baselines keep the typing motion,
alter appearance (LiveEdit, OmniVideo2), or change the viewpoint (Bernini),
whereas VADD stops the typing and places the woman's hands on her lower
back while preserving the scene; it likewise makes the mother lean in to
help her daughter draw. Appendix~\ref{sec:additional-qualitative} shows
more cases.
